title: Where Joy Breeds
---

You'd think that we'd find joy when we finish a project. The finished wood project, the completed writing project, the vacation. But joy slips away so easily. 

Where does joy breed? In the tiny moments of life where we are in relationship to the stuff of life. Look for it in the scent of the morning air, the sound of the dog snoring, the contact of bum-to-chair, the taste of coffee or the sight of the pill jars on the counter. Look for it in her face, not as a fixed object but as a changing, living mystery. Look for it in the scent of cut wood, the knife moving through an onion, the strange fact that your hands still work.

Joy becomes a way of looking; a practiced way of attending to reality. 

Joy is a method of attention. Attention is a form of affection. This makes joy a form of affection, and attention is a form of love. Simply put, joy is a form of love. 

Joy is not something special. It is ordinary and available wherever we look. 

Everything collides in a joyful moment. The breath, a song, the scent of coffee, laughter in the next room, and the surprise that everything is colliding at once.

Joy breeds with quiet attention to life's interstitial moments, if we look.
